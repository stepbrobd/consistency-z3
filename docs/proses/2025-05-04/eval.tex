\section{Evaluation}

We evaluated \sysname{} using three representative case studies to
demonstrate its effectiveness in checking compatibility between
applications and compositions of distributed systems.
Our evaluation focuses on two key metrics: the framework's ability to
find valid compositions with minimal semantic score and its
performance characteristics under different system complexities.

For each case study, we enumerated all possible combinations of
consistency models for nodes with non-null provs fields, while
keeping nodes with predefined needs fields unchanged. We assigned
scores to individual consistency models and their combinations based
on their strength as shown in Figure \ref{fig:semantics-tree}.

All experiments were conducted on an Intel i7-1360P processor with
64GB RAM running NixOS 25.11 (Linux 6.12.30).
We used Z3 version 4.15.1 as our SMT solver backend with a 10 seconds
timeout limit. However, during our evaluation, we discovered a
termination bug in Z3 4.15.1
where the solver would not respect timeout parameters, continuing to
run well beyond the specified limit (e.g., still running after 2
minutes despite a 10-second timeout). To address this, we implemented
a watchdog mechanism using strace to monitors write systemcalls to
stdout, sending SIGINT if the 10-second timeout is exceeded,
following the philosophy established in recent
verification work Verus that ``it's better to improve solver
performance than rely on a slow proof'' \cite{verus}.
% https://github.com/verus-lang/verus/blob/0b6f3cbb8fceff6681b4988c3cac37a8b801f8e6/source/docs/guide/src/smt_failures.md

The shopping mall case study models an e-commerce platform with three
configurable nodes: arbitrator, transaction processor, and shop
service. We evaluated all
$17^3$ possible combinations of consistency models across these
nodes. The evaluation completed in 251.77 seconds with an average
duration of 5.12 ms per combination and 0 timeouts.

This case study models a complex media platform with five database
nodes: user database, metadata database, rental database, review
database, and video database.
The full evaluation space contains
$17^5$ combinations. At the time of writing, we have completed
800,507 combinations (56.38\% of the total space) in 131,563.92
seconds (\~36.55 hours), with an average duration of 16.44 ms per
combination and no timeouts.
Our tool was able to find the composition in our original design in
about 20.43 seconds.
This case study represents the most complex scenario in our
evaluation, testing the framework's scalability with realistic
distributed system topologies.

This case study represents a service chain with four services (SVC1
-> SVC2 -> SVC3 -> SVC4) where causal consistency must be maintained
across the entire chain. We evaluated all
$17^4$ combinations, completing the analysis in 14,337.11 seconds (\~3.98 hours)
with an average duration of 17.17 ms per combination.
Our tool successfully identified the composition in our original design
in about 261.09 seconds.
The timeout rate result arout 0.15\%, with non-timeout instances
averaging 15.65 ms.
